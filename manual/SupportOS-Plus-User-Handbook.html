About this book

How to use this handbook

This handbook describes SupportOS+, version 1.8.0 — a desktop program for customer-support teams. It explains what the program does, how to use every one of its pages, what to type into every field, and where each value you need actually comes from. Nothing in this book assumes prior technical knowledge: every term is explained the first time it appears, and every instruction is written as a numbered step you can follow on screen.

Everything in these pages was taken directly from the program itself — its screens, its buttons, its settings and its on-screen messages. If this book quotes a label, that is exactly the wording you will see in the application. Where the book says save credentials, you will find a button with precisely that text.

The chapters are arranged in the order you will naturally need them: getting the program installed (Chapters 1–4), connecting it to Help Scout and your first data sync (Chapters 5–6), the everyday work of answering customers (Chapters 7–15), measuring and improving your support (Chapters 16–19), automation and AI (Chapters 20–22), and finally the system pages, your data files, and a troubleshooting guide (Chapters 23–26).

If you read only one chapter, read Chapter 5, Connecting Help Scout — it is the one step that turns an empty application into a working help desk. Chapter 2, Words this app uses, is a dictionary of the terms used everywhere else; return to it whenever a word is unfamiliar.

Conventions used in this book
  • Words shown like Sync now are exact button and field labels as they appear on screen.
  • Numbered step lists are meant to be followed in order, from top to bottom.
  • Tables titled What to enter / Where it comes from tell you, field by field, what value belongs in each box — and which website or menu that value comes from.
  • Boxes marked Good to know contain background explanations; boxes marked Watch out warn about actions that cannot be undone.

Chapter 1

What is SupportOS+?

A short answer, then a slightly longer one — and a ten-minute quick start.

The short answer. SupportOS+ is a program that runs on your own computer and brings your company's Help Scout help desk onto your desk. It copies your conversations, customers and help articles into a private database stored on your machine, lets you read and answer everything from one clean window, and adds helpful extras: searching, reports, a knowledge base, automation rules and optional AI assistance that also runs on your own computer.

The longer answer. Most help desks live on a website: you log in through a browser, the company that sells the help desk keeps the data, and you see whatever that website shows you. SupportOS+ takes a different approach. Once you connect it to your Help Scout account, it pulls a complete copy of your support history into a database file on your own computer. From then on, reading, searching and reporting are instant, because everything is local. When you write a reply, it is delivered to Help Scout so your customers receive it exactly as before — SupportOS+ simply gives you a faster, calmer place to work. It never deletes anything on Help Scout by itself, it shows honest status messages for everything it does, and it does not send any telemetry anywhere.

Two ideas are worth remembering throughout this book. First, nothing is simulated: the app starts completely empty, and everything you see after that comes from your real Help Scout account. Second, your data stays yours: the database, the backups and the AI all live on your machine unless you deliberately connect an online AI service of your own choosing.

1.1What you need before you start

  • A computer running Linux — SupportOS+ is a desktop program for Linux. Installing it takes one command or one double-click (Chapter 3).
  • A Help Scout account with API access — the Free plan of Help Scout does not include API access; any paid plan does. If you are unsure, ask whoever manages your Help Scout billing. You will also need permission to create a "custom app" in Help Scout (Chapter 5 shows exactly where).
  • Optional: LM Studio — a free program that runs AI models on your own computer. Without it, SupportOS+ still does everything except the AI helpers, and it will remind you politely. Chapter 21 walks through it step by step.

1.2Quick start in ten minutes

Here is the whole journey in miniature. Each step points to the chapter that explains it fully.

  1. Install SupportOS+ and start it (Chapter 3). You will see an empty Dashboard — that is normal and honest: there is no sample data.
  2. Open Settings in the left sidebar, choose the Help Scout tab, and paste your client ID and client secret from your Help Scout custom app (Chapter 5 shows where to create one).
  3. Press save credentials. The app connects immediately, runs five automatic connection checks, and starts pulling your history by itself.
  4. Wait for the first sync to finish — on large accounts this can take an hour or more; the Sync Health page shows live progress (Chapter 6).
  5. Open the Inbox and start answering. Replies you send are delivered to Help Scout automatically (Chapters 7–9).
  6. Optional but recommended: set up a small "webhook relay" so live chats and customer ratings arrive in real time (Chapter 5.5), and switch on the AI helpers with LM Studio (Chapter 21).
Good to know

SupportOS+ shows its version number in the bottom-left corner of the window, next to the name. This handbook matches version 1.8.0. The app can check for newer versions whenever you press check for updates in Settings — it looks at the public release list once, only when you click, and sends nothing about you.

Chapter 2

Words this app uses

A plain-English dictionary. Every term below is used throughout the app and throughout this book.

Support desk software has its own vocabulary, and SupportOS+ uses the same words as Help Scout so nothing is confusingly renamed. Here are the terms you will meet, in the order they matter.

Help Scout — the online help desk service your company already uses. Customers write to your support email address, and Help Scout turns each message into a conversation. SupportOS+ connects to it and mirrors your data locally.
Conversation (or ticket) — one exchange with one customer about one topic. It has a number (like #778), a subject, a status, and a thread of messages.
Thread / thread entry — one individual message inside a conversation: a customer's email, an agent's reply, an internal note, a phone-log entry, and so on.
Mailbox — a folder in Help Scout for a stream of conversations, typically one per support address (for example support@ and billing@). A conversation always lives in exactly one mailbox.
Status — where a conversation stands: active (being worked on), pending (waiting for the customer), closed (finished) or spam.
Tag — a short label attached to a conversation, such as refund or bug. Tags are used for filtering, reports and automation.
Customer — a person in your address book: name, email addresses, phone numbers, organization, and the history of their conversations.
Organization — a company that one or more customers belong to, recognized by its website domains (like acme.com).
Mirror / local mirror — the private copy of your Help Scout data that SupportOS+ keeps in a database file on your computer. "The mirror" is what the app reads when you browse, search and run reports.
Sync — the process of updating the mirror: pulling new and changed conversations, customers, and everything else from Help Scout. The first sync copies your whole history; after that, small frequent syncs keep it current.
Webhook — a small automatic notification that Help Scout sends to the app the moment something happens (a new message, a rating, a chat). Webhooks make updates arrive in near real time. They need the optional "relay" described in Chapter 5.5.
Credentials — the pair of secret codes (a client ID and a client secret) that identifies SupportOS+ to Help Scout, plus an optional webhook secret and Docs API key. Chapter 5 explains each one and where to get it.
OAuth — the standard sign-in handshake between two programs. You normally do not need to think about it: the recommended "client credentials" path signs in without opening a browser.
Docs API / Help Center — the part of Help Scout that stores your public help articles, organized in collections. With a Docs API key, SupportOS+ can mirror those articles and even publish approved drafts to them.
AI model / LM Studio — an AI text engine. LM Studio is a free desktop program that runs such models on your own computer; SupportOS+ can use it for summaries, suggested replies, translation and more. No model, no problem — the app works without it.
Embedding / vector index — the technology that lets search also find things by meaning, not just by matching words. It runs quietly in the background once an embedding model is set up (Chapter 21).
SLA — "service level agreement": the target time for first response and for resolution, measured in business hours for each mailbox.
Data directory — the folder on your computer where SupportOS+ keeps its database, backups and settings: ~/.local/share/supportos-plus by default. Chapter 25 lists every file in it.
Good to know

When this book says locally or local, it always means "on your computer, inside the data directory". When it says remote or on the platform, it means "on Help Scout's servers". The app is careful to always tell you which side an action happened on (for example, a successful reply reports Reply delivered to Help Scout).

Two ways to install, what you see on first launch, and how updating works.

  1. A window opens, titled SupportOS+, about 1180 by 760 pixels in size (you can resize it freely).
  2. The app quietly creates its data folder — ~/.local/share/supportos-plus — with the database, a key file for stored secrets, and empty folders for backups and attachments. Chapter 25 explains every file.
  3. You land on the Dashboard, which is honestly empty. The subtitle on many pages will remind you: the app shows only your real data, never samples.
  4. The Onboarding page (bottom of the sidebar, in the System group) shows a short checklist of what is left to do. It is a helpful home base while you set things up.

3.3One copy at a time

SupportOS+ keeps all its information in one database, so only one copy of the program may use it at a time. If you accidentally start a second copy while one is already running, the second copy shows a message saying that another SupportOS+ instance is already running and then closes itself. Nothing is harmed — just switch to the window that is already open.

3.4Checking for updates

Open Settings → Sync & AI tab and find the card About & updates. It shows the line Running version: v1.8.0 and a check for updates button. Pressing it asks the public SupportOS+ release list once whether a newer version exists, and answers with one of three phrases: up to date, an update is available, or running ahead of the latest published release. The app never checks by itself and never sends any information about you or your data.

Good to know

To close the app, simply close the window — everything is saved continuously as you work. If the program ever fails to start (for example, because another program is using the network port it needs), it shows a desktop pop-up titled SupportOS+ failed to start with the reason, and writes the technical details to a file called logs/fatal.log inside the data directory. Chapter 26 explains what to do with it.

Chapter 5

Connecting Help Scout

The most important chapter in this book: where each secret comes from, how the connection is verified, and how live updates reach the app.

To pull your data, SupportOS+ needs permission to talk to your Help Scout account. That permission comes from four values, entered once on the Settings page → Help Scout tab. Two are mandatory (the client ID and client secret), and two are optional extras (the webhook secret and the Docs API key). This chapter shows where to obtain each one, what the app does with them, and how to verify that everything works.

5.1Creating the custom app in Help Scout

The client ID and client secret live in a "custom app" that you create once inside Help Scout. Think of it as issuing a staff badge for SupportOS+: it identifies the program and grants it access, and you can revoke it any time.

  1. Open helpscout.com in your browser and sign in to your account.
  2. Click your profile picture in the bottom-left corner of Help Scout.
  3. Choose Apps & Integrations from the menu.
  4. Create a custom app (the page offers this when you add a new app). Help Scout will generate two long strings: the Client ID and the Client Secret.
  5. Leave the Help Scout browser tab open — you will paste both strings into SupportOS+ in a moment.
Good to know — plans and permissions

Help Scout's Free plan does not include API access, so the connection will be refused on it; any paid plan works. The user whose credentials you enter must be a full user (not a "Light User") and must be able to see the mailboxes you want to sync. If the Connection Doctor later reports a mailbox problem, this is the first thing to check in Help Scout.

5.2The four values, and where each comes from

On the Help Scout tab in Settings, scroll to the card Help Scout credentials. Each field masks what you type (there is a show secrets checkbox if you want to verify). Here is the full field reference:

Press save credentials when done. The values are stored encrypted on this computer (Chapter 25 explains how), and the connection activates immediately — no restart needed. Right after saving, the app runs its Connection Doctor (Section 5.4) and the first sync starts by itself. If anything is wrong, a red toast names the failing step. The three secret fields are emptied again after saving as a safety habit; the client ID stays visible. Each field also shows a small badge telling you where the current value came from: not set, environment (set by a technically-minded colleague on this computer) or app settings (entered here). Press clear stored if you ever want to remove the saved values — the existing connection tokens are untouched until you disconnect.

5.3Connecting — the easy way and the advanced way

With the client ID and secret saved, press the primary button connect with client credentials (recommended). This signs the app in directly — no browser, no extra steps. On success you will see connected with client credentials — tokens stored. The sync pulls your account automatically. The status badge at the top of the card changes to connected (green).

There is also an advanced, rarely-needed path hidden behind advanced: browser authorization flow: it opens the Help Scout sign-in page in your browser and redirects back to the app when you approve. It only works if the exact callback address shown as redirect uri: is registered on your Help Scout custom app, and it depends on the local listener (next section) being reachable. The app itself recommends the simple path if the browser flow fails. After approving in the browser you will see a page reading Connected to Help Scout — You can close this tab and return to SupportOS.

To sign out completely, press disconnect. It drops the stored sign-in tokens after a confirmation; all local data is fully preserved, and you can reconnect later.

5.4The Connection Doctor — five automatic checks

The card Connection Doctor verifies the whole chain from your computer to Help Scout. It runs automatically after saving credentials, and you can re-run it any time with run the checks while a connection is active. Each step appears as a line marked ✓ ok, ! warning or ✗ failed, with a remedy written in plain language when something is wrong:

  1. Reach the Help Scout API — can this computer reach Help Scout's servers at all? Failure remedies mention your internet connection, VPN or firewall.
  2. Sign in with client credentials — are the client ID and secret correct? The remedy reminds you to check for stray spaces and that the app is enabled (and that Free plans cannot connect).
  3. Read the connected user — confirms the signed-in account is a full user with API access.
  4. List mailboxes — counts how many mailboxes the connection can see, and warns honestly if the account has none visible.
  5. Rate-limit budget — reports how many requests per minute Help Scout allows and how many remain.

The doctor stops at the first hard failure, so the remedy you see always belongs to the step that actually broke — follow it from top to bottom.

5.5Webhooks and the relay — real-time updates

Syncing by polling (the app asking Help Scout "anything new?") runs automatically and is enough for reading and replying. Three kinds of updates, however, can only arrive by push notification: live chats, customer satisfaction ratings, and instant per-conversation updates the moment something changes. To receive them, Help Scout must be able to send messages to your computer — and a desktop app sitting behind normal home or office internet is not directly reachable from outside.

The solution is a small helper called a relay (a tunnel) that bridges the two. The app documents this directly on the Settings page, in the card Webhook relay & health:

  1. Run a tunnel on this computer that forwards a public web address to the app's private listener. The app names three common tools: cloudflared tunnel --url http://127.0.0.1:PORT, ngrok http 127.0.0.1:PORT, or ssh -N -R 80:127.0.0.1:PORT serveo.net. Whoever manages your computers can set this up in minutes; the app's own listener runs on port 3000 by default (the Settings page shows the actual port in its line loopback listener answering on :3000…).
  2. Register a webhook in your Help Scout custom app pointing at that public address, ending in /webhook, and paste the same webhook secret you stored in Section 5.2.
  3. Paste the public address into the field public relay URL: in Settings and press save relay URL.
  4. Press check now (or wait — the app re-checks about every six hours by itself). The verdict appears with a coloured badge: healthy (green), reregistered (amber — the app noticed Help Scout had dropped the registration and fixed it), or failed (red).
Good to know — the 401 trick

The Settings page includes a neat self-test: a properly wired relay answers any test knock on the door with a rejection — 401 (signature check) — because the knocker did not present the secret. In other words, "access denied" is the sound of success here: it proves the public address reaches the app. If you get anything else, the relay is not reaching the app yet.

5.6When something goes wrong

  • "loopback listener NOT running" — the app's internal receiver did not start; webhooks and the browser sign-in are unavailable. Restart SupportOS+ and check for a port conflict (Chapter 26).
  • "Help Scout rejected the login credentials" — the client ID/secret pair is wrong, has a stray space, or the app is disabled. Re-check Section 5.1.
  • Connection works, but no mailboxes — the Help Scout user cannot see any mailbox; check mailbox assignments in Help Scout.
  • Help Center sync idle — the Docs API key is missing; the warning names exactly where to get one (Section 5.2).
Chapter 6

Your first sync & Sync Health

What happens during the big first pull, how the app keeps itself up to date, and how to read the Sync Health page.

As soon as the connection is active, SupportOS+ begins copying your Help Scout history. This chapter explains what that feels like, how long it takes, and where to watch progress.

6.1The first sync

The first run copies everything, in a fixed order: account settings, users, teams, mailboxes, folders, tags, mailbox fields, organizations, customers, saved replies, workflows, conversations with all their messages, and (with a Docs API key) your Help Center collections and articles. Progress is shown on the Sync Health page as lines like progress: customers · resource 12/19 · 4812 record(s) so far.

How long will it take? The app itself says it best: large accounts can take "an hour or more". Leave the app running until it finishes — closing it just pauses the work, and the app will resume safely next time. When it finishes, Diagnostics records Initial sync finished: 12,481 records processed, 0 resource(s) failed.

6.2After the first sync — automatic freshness

Once the mirror is complete, the app keeps it current with no effort on your part:

  • While the account is busy, the app checks for changes roughly every 30 seconds.
  • When things are quiet, it gradually slows down to save effort, at most once per the interval set in Settings (default: one minute).
  • The moment you send something — a reply, a note, a status change — the app immediately checks for updates, so your sent message and any answer appear quickly.
  • Webhooks (if you set up the relay) deliver individual changes in near real time, on top of the regular checking.

You can also nudge it manually: the Sync now button appears on the Dashboard, Inbox, Customers, Organizations and Reports pages. When it finishes, a toast reports the outcome, including the honest case Up to date — nothing new from Help Scout.

6.3Reading the Sync Health page

Open Sync Health from the System group. The page shows:

  • Manual run buttons at the top: Run initial sync (the full pull), Run incremental sync (a normal catch-up), Run reconcile (a careful comparison that also notices conversations deleted or merged on Help Scout), and Cancel running sync (stops a run mid-flight; it resumes cleanly later). Guards prevent accidents: starting a second run reports A sync is already running.
  • The Engine card — the app's one-word summary of where syncing stands: NEW (never synced), INITIALIZING (the big first pull), LIVE (healthy and current), or CATCHING_UP (some resources failed and are being retried). It also shows the webhook queue counters and the latest webhook health verdict.
  • Checkpoints — one row per data type showing when it was last synced successfully and whether anything failed.
  • Recent runs — a table of past runs with their type, state, and record counts.
Good to know — Onboarding status

The Onboarding page shows a compact version of the same information: whether credentials exist, how many conversations are mirrored, and the sync state in three friendly words: new (never synced) → registered (a sync has run) → receiving (live webhook events are arriving). Its Run initial sync button is the same one as on Sync Health, and Mark complete simply checks off the welcome list.

Chapter 7

Inbox: finding conversations

Filters, saved status views, search and drill-downs — how to put the right conversation in front of you.

The Inbox is where you will spend your day. It is split in two: a list of conversations on the left, and the full detail of the selected conversation on the right. This chapter covers the list side; Chapters 8 and 9 cover the detail side.

7.1The view chips and filters

At the top of the page sit four one-click views: All, Active, Pending and Closed — the four conversation statuses. Below them, three dropdowns narrow the list further:

  • Mailbox — default All mailboxes; pick one to focus on a single support address.
  • Channel — All channels, Email or Chat.
  • Tag — Any tag or one specific tag.

Press apply to use the dropdown picks, or reset filters to clear everything at once. The subtitle under the page title always tells you how many conversations match — for example 1–50 of 214 conversations — and the ‹ prev / next › buttons page through the list, 50 rows at a time.

7.2Searching for one conversation

To the right of the filters is a search box with the placeholder search conversations… plus a small number field marked #. The number field jumps straight to a conversation by its number — type 778 or #778, press the search button, and conversation 778 appears. The text box searches subjects and message contents; results respect the filters you have set. Pressing Enter in either field runs the search.

7.3Reading a list row

Each row in the list shows, top to bottom: the conversation number and subject (like #778  Refund not received) with a coloured status badge; the customer's name and the mailbox it belongs to; and a short preview of the newest message with its date and time. Rows whose conversation could not be loaded show an honest error instead of silence.

7.4Arriving from Operations

When you click a workload tile on the Operations page (Chapter 12), the Inbox opens with a coloured banner at the top reading Operations drill-down: Customer waiting and the list pre-filtered to exactly those conversations. An × on the banner clears the drill-down and returns you to your normal filters.

If the list is empty

With no connection configured, the Inbox explains itself: "Help Scout is not connected — nothing can sync yet. Add your app credentials in Settings (Help Scout tab), press connect, and this inbox fills automatically." If you are connected and still see nothing, check that the filters are not hiding your conversations — reset filters is the quickest test.

Chapter 8

Inbox: reading & acting

The thread, the action card, triage chips and the conversation's AI briefing — everything around the message you selected.

8.1The thread

Select a conversation on the left and its full history appears in the Thread card on the right: every message in order, each marked with a coloured badge identifying its kind — Customer for customer messages, Agent for sent replies, Note for internal notes, plus Chat, Phone and AI where applicable. Above the thread, the conversation's title line shows the number, subject, status badge, the customer's name and email, the mailbox, and the priority if one is set.

8.2The action card — status and assignment

Near the composer sits the action card, the steering wheel of the conversation:

  • status: four chips — active, pending, closed, spam. Clicking one changes the status locally and sends the change to Help Scout.
  • assign: a dropdown of teammates (showing the current assignee, or unassigned) with an assign button next to it.

Every action shows its outcome on the spot, in green or red: Reply accepted — delivering to Help Scout (watch the status line). or, for failures, the exact reason. A small live status line then tracks the delivery: queued → sending → delivered to Help Scout ✓ — or failed: with the reason.

9.3How delivery works

Pressing send does not block you: the message is handed to a fast background queue — user sends always go first — and delivered to Help Scout within seconds. The status line under the composer narrates each step (queued for delivery… → sending to Help Scout… → delivered ✓), and a toast confirms it. 9.4One setting every agent should know

In Settings → Sync & AI, the field status after reply: decides what happens to a conversation the moment you reply. Three choices, shown exactly like this:

  • pending (await customer) — the default: after you answer, the ball is in the customer's court.
  • open (await agent) — keep it on your side of the desk after replying.
  • keep current status — replies never move the status at all.

Replies and notes accept normal text formatting, and messages that arrive with formatting (bold, links, lists) are cleaned automatically before display: anything unsafe is reduced to plain text, so the thread is always safe to read. Links in customer emails always open in a new tab.

Above the typing area sits a row of one-click helpers:

  • Review draft — runs the "Draft coaching" checks (next section) on what you have written.
  • Shorten, Expand, Warmer — rewrites your draft at three different dials, using the Draft AI role when one is routed. If you edit the text while a rewrite is running, the app notices and keeps your version, telling you so.
  • Translate — a language dropdown (18 languages, from Spanish to Swedish) plus a Translate chip that rewrites the draft in the chosen language, translated locally and cached.

While any of these runs, the chip shows AI working…; results are reported in the line beneath, including the honest failure The AI transform produced no result — the feature is off or no Draft role endpoint is routed.

10.5Draft coaching — ten gentle checks

Pressing Review draft opens the Draft coaching card. Its subtitle sets the tone: "advisory — never blocks sending". It runs up to ten checks over your draft and flags anything worth a second look; each finding carries advice and the evidence it is based on:

  • Unanswered customer questions — questions in the thread your draft does not appear to answer.
  • Duplicated questions — you asking the customer something they already answered.
  • Unsupported claims — statements the AI layer cannot trace to evidence (needs a routed AI role).
  • Unsupported timeframe — promising "by tomorrow" when no known issue supports that.
  • Missing acknowledgment — diving straight in without acknowledging what the customer said.
  • Excessive wording and Insufficient detail — two dials on length, compared against the customer's own style.
  • Internal information leakage — wording that reads like it slipped from an internal note.
  • Wrong customer context — mentioning a different customer's details.
  • Communication preference mismatch — e.g. a very technical answer to a non-technical customer.

The card summarises the result as badges like 2 flagged and 9 checks ran, and every check reports pass or flagged — nothing is ever hidden, and nothing is ever changed without you.

Assigning and changing status always wait for a human decision, and setting priority waits when the priority is urgent; adding tags and posting notes run by themselves. Waiting items collect in the card Pending approvals (and in the Operations tile), each showing the rule, the conversation and the action, with approve and reject buttons. Rejected actions simply do nothing; every run of every rule is recorded with its outcome.

Chapter 21

AI Center & LM Studio

Setting up local AI in plain steps, the six tabs of the AI Center, and the switches that keep it polite.

The AI Center is where AI is configured, observed and — when needed — switched off. It assumes nothing: every feature degrades gracefully without a model, and a single global switch disables all AI at once.

21.1Installing LM Studio — the plain-English version

LM Studio is a free desktop program (from lmstudio.ai) that runs AI models on your own computer. SupportOS+ talks to it over your machine's local network — nothing leaves your computer. The whole setup:

  1. Install LM Studio on the same computer as SupportOS+ and open it.
  2. In LM Studio, download a chat model (for example Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) and an embedding model (for example bge-m3) from its model catalogue, and load them.
  3. In LM Studio's Local Server tab, press Start. The default address it serves is http://127.0.0.1:1234.
  4. In SupportOS+, open Settings → LM Studio and fill three fields: base url: (hint shows http://localhost:1234/v1), chat model: and embedding model: — the names exactly as LM Studio lists them. Press save.
  5. Verify in AI Center → Health: press test connection and look for connected · N model(s) in green.

21.2Health — endpoints, keys and roles

The Health tab is the control room. Its cards, in order:

  • LM Studio settings — a read-only summary of the current setup, with the test connection button and its three possible answers: connected with the model count, connected but no models loaded, or offline with the reason.
  • Provider hub — endpoints — the list of AI services the app knows: the built-in LM Studio (local), an optional Ollama (local), and any cloud services you add yourself. Each endpoint has a probe button (checks it is reachable and counts its models), a set key button for cloud services (the key is stored in the app's encrypted secret store, never in plain text), and a remove button. The collapsing add endpoint (cloud BYOK) form adds a cloud service with its name, web address, API key and the prices per million tokens it charges (used only for the cost estimates below).
  • Model-role routing — the cleverest card. Instead of one model for everything, the app thinks in five roles, and you route each role to any endpoint and model:
RoleWhat it doesUsed by

  • Cost ledger — last 7 days — a per-feature, per-day table of runs, tokens and estimated cost (cloud prices only; local models cost electricity).

21.3Analytics and Attributes

Analytics shows how good the AI has been at its job: how many tickets were analysed, the success rate, how often drafts were accepted versus rejected, and how many AI claims were unsupported. Attributes shows the vocabulary the AI has learned to attach to conversations — intent, urgency, frustration, technical familiarity and ten more — with the distribution of values and a grounding rule printed in the card: a missing attribute reads as unknown and is never invented. Attributes are local-only and never written back to Help Scout.

21.4Usage and Jobs

Usage keeps the receipts: every AI run of the last 30 days with its model, token counts, duration and estimated cost; the draft feedback statistics (accepted, rejected, how much people edited before sending); and the golden set — nine built-in test cases that the app runs through the analysis model at the press of Run golden set now, scoring whether the AI still passes its own exam. Jobs lists the raw run log: type, status, conversation, model, when.

21.5Copilot — the standing chat

The Copilot tab is the free-standing version of "Ask the assistant": saved chat sessions, a message log with citations, and the same 22 read-only tools. Ask things like Summarize the last three conversations or What documentation applies? — the agent searches your mirror, quotes what it used, and says plainly when it found nothing. It cannot send, change or delete anything.

Good to know — the off switch philosophy

Every AI feature is designed to fail soft: disable a model, the endpoint, the feature, or all of AI, and the app keeps working — search falls back to plain matching, drafts fall back to the built-in assistant, digests keep their facts without the narrative. Nothing breaks; the app simply tells you which mode it is in.

Chapter 22

Outreach: campaigns & segments

Polite bulk email to people who already talk to you — with guard rails where it matters.

The Outreach page sends email campaigns to groups of existing customers, with three tabs: Campaigns, Segments and Do not contact.

22.1Segments — defining "who"

A segment is a saved definition of a group of customers. Press New Segment, give it a name:, and describe the group in the criteria (JSON): box — a short structured sentence in the form { "combinator": "all", "conditions": [ … ], "exclude": [ … ] }. In practice: give this form to whoever maintains your filters; the app validates it on save and refuses malformed definitions with a precise error. Conditions can express things like "has an email address", "total tickets is at least 2", "was contacted within the last 90 days", or membership in an organization. A segment is required before any campaign can be created — campaigns can never target "everyone" by accident.

22.2Campaigns — the lifecycle

Press New Campaign and fill the form: name:, subject:, the body: (which may use personalisation placeholders — see below), a mailbox id: (the numeric id of the mailbox the sends go from) and a segment id: (shown in the saved-segments hint on the same card). After create campaign, the campaign card offers the five lifecycle buttons, each state-checked:

  • queue — from draft or paused: one conversation per recipient is prepared and sending begins.
  • pause / resume — stop and continue; already-sent recipients are never touched twice.
  • cancel remaining — discards the not-yet-sent sends (with its confirmation dialog); sent ones stay sent.
  • retry failed — re-queues only the failed recipients (each recipient gets at most three attempts in total).

Six personalisation placeholders work in the body: {{first_name}}, {{last_name}}, {{company}}, {{organization}}, {{last_ticket_number}} and {{last_ticket_subject}} — anything a recipient has no value for renders as empty, and the validation report warns you about it. The Campaign report card totals everything: sent, failed, skipped, replied.

22.3Do not contact

The Do not contact tab is the permanent unsubscribe list. Add a customer by their numeric customer id: with an optional reason:; every campaign automatically skips everyone on this list, and the campaign report shows how many were skipped for it. Removing someone requires the confirmation dialog, whose wording makes the consequence plain: the customer becomes contactable by every campaign again.

23.1Notifications

The Notifications page collects everything that concerns you personally: customer replies, assignments, @-mentions, SLA warnings, automation approvals, AI escalations, sync failures and more — fifteen kinds in all, all enabled by default. Three tabs sort them: All, Mentions and Unread; a counter in the title shows 24 total · 3 unread; and mark all read clears the board. 23.2Custom Objects

Custom Objects are the app's built-in "build your own list" tool: define a type (say, Refund request or Returned device), give it fields, then store records under it and link them to your work.

  1. Press New Type. Fill name: and description:, then add fields with + add field. Press create type.
  2. Select the type on the left. On the right, the card New object asks for an object title and then one input per field you defined (numbers and true/false values are validated on the spot). Press create object.
  3. Objects appear as cards with their field values; search objects… finds them, delete removes one (with confirmation).

Connectors attach outside data as read-only tables the app (and, if you allow it, the AI tools) can look at: an http endpoint or a csv file placed inside the app's data directory. Press New Connector, pick the kind, give a name:, and either a url: or a file (data dir): like billing.csv. Set refresh seconds: (between 60 and 86,400 — one minute to one day) and tick AI tools may query only if the data is fit for the assistant to read. Chapter 25

Your data & privacy

Where everything lives, what is protected, what leaves your machine — and how to move or wipe it.

25.1One folder holds everything

SupportOS+ keeps 100% of its data in a single folder — by default ~/.local/share/supportos-plus (a different location can be chosen with the SPP_DATA_DIR setting by whoever manages your computers). Copy that one folder and you have copied the entire workspace. Its contents:

File or folderWhat it is
supportos-plus.dbThe entire workspace: every conversation, customer, article, report and setting (plus its two quiet helpers, the -wal and -shm files). Copy all three together, with the app closed.
secrets.keyThe encryption key for stored passwords and tokens (Help Scout credentials, sign-in tokens, connector keys). Private and protected — treat it like a house key.
backups/Automatic and manual backups, plus the JSON/CSV exports and the move-to-another-computer bundles.
vectors/The AI search index (rebuildable; removed by the workspace reset).
attachments/Downloaded email attachments from conversations.
logs/fatal.logA crash log written only if the app ever fails to start or panics. The file to send someone helping you (Chapter 26).
knowledge-import/The in-box for knowledge documents (Chapter 14.1) — the only folder the app reads imports from.
instance.lockThe "one copy at a time" marker. Harmless if left behind after a crash.

25.2What is protected, and how

Your Help Scout credentials, sign-in tokens, connector keys and webhook secret are stored encrypted (industry-standard AES-256-GCM) under the secrets.key file, which itself is locked to your user account (permission 0600 — readable only by you). The rest of the database — conversations, customer details, documents — is stored in ordinary readable form, protected by the same file permissions; that is precisely why backups and exports are also locked to 0600, and why the folder deserves normal computer care (disk encryption if your machine travels, for instance).

25.3Backups — automatic and manual

  • Automatic — about once a day (every 24 hours by default, adjustable from 1 hour upward; 0 turns it off), the app writes a full backup named like supportos-backup-2026-10-24T12-34-56-789Z.db into backups/, plus a small sidecar file with the settings (passwords removed). Only the newest twenty backups are kept; older ones are pruned automatically.
  • Manual — the create backup button writes one immediately and tells you where.
  • Restore — the two-step restore (Chapter 24.5) verifies the backup file's integrity before touching anything, and refuses clearly if a file is not a genuine SupportOS backup. Your current data is moved aside and replaced only after the check passes; the app restarts fresh from the restored copy.

25.4Exports and moving to another computer

  • export JSON — a readable snapshot of conversations, customers, organizations, knowledge and AI drafts. The app warns that it contains customer data and should be handled carefully.
  • export conversations CSV — a spreadsheet of every conversation: number, subject, status, mailbox, customer, dates.
  • Encrypted bundle — for moving the whole mirror to another machine: a password-protected file (minimum eight characters; the password is used to derive the key, so there is no recovery without it). The wrong password changes nothing, and the app verifies the bundle's contents before importing — writing a safety backup of the previous data first.

25.5What leaves your computer

  • To Help Scout: your replies, notes, status and assignment changes — the work you do — plus the normal reading of your account during sync. Nothing else.
  • To AI: by default, nothing (everything runs locally). If you add a cloud AI endpoint, the texts of the features you route to it go to that service, under your account.
  • To anyone else: nothing. No telemetry, no analytics, no automatic update checks — the update check runs only when you click it, against the public release list.
  • Deletion, done properly: the workspace reset (Chapter 24.2) does not merely delete — it tells the database to zero the freed space and then compacts the file, so deleted records are not recoverable from it afterwards.

26.1First aid: the three places to look

  1. The toast or status line — the app announces every failure in the corner, with the reason. Reading it usually answers the question.
  2. The Diagnostics page — the full history: every error and warning, the outcome of every sync run, and any delivery that failed, all filterable. The clear log button empties it (with confirmation) when it gets long.
  3. The logs folder — if the app will not even start, the file ~/.local/share/supportos-plus/logs/fatal.log records what happened; send it to whoever helps you.

26.3Frequently asked questions

Do I need the internet? For pulling from and sending to Help Scout, yes. For everything else — reading your history, searching, reports, knowledge, the local AI — no: the app works fully offline and tells you when remote actions are unavailable.

Can two people share one database? Not simultaneously — the app enforces one instance per data folder. Use the encrypted bundle (Chapter 25.4) to move a copy to another machine.

Where did my data go after I deleted a rule/article/object? Confirmation dialogs promised it: those deletions are permanent, locally and (for saved replies) on Help Scout too. Backups from before the deletion are the only way back.

How do I start completely over? Settings → Help Scout → Workspace data (Chapter 24.2): it wipes the local mirror, keeps your setup, and re-pulls the account fresh on the next start.

Which Help Scout plan do I need? Any paid plan for the connection itself; Plus or Pro for the official Reports API tab; the Docs API key for Help Center features.

Does the AI send anything to customers? Never. It drafts, reviews and rewrites — every send is a human press of the button (and the app protects even that with duplicate protection).

How do I change the look? The sun/moon switch in the sidebar or header. Light and dark are both first-class and remembered.

Something behaves oddly and this book does not cover it. Open Diagnostics, note the exact message, and check the Sync Health page — between those two, the app almost always tells you precisely what it needs.
